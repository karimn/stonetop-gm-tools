# Player moves — full reference

Distilled mechanics and adjudication notes for every player move in Stonetop Book I. Paraphrased from the SRD; see the [full text](https://github.com/karimn/stonetop-srd/blob/main/book_one/player-moves.md) for verbatim wording and worked examples.

All basic moves resolve 2d6 + stat unless noted: **10+** strong hit, **7-9** weak hit, **6-** miss (mark XP, GM makes a hard move).

## Basic moves

### Defy Danger

**Trigger:** Danger looms, stakes are high, and the action is chancy — but check first whether a more specific move applies (it almost always does when a foe or a ranged shot is involved).

**Roll:** +STR (power through), +DEX (speed/agility/finesse), +CON (endure/hold steady), +INT (expertise/clever plan), +WIS (willpower/senses), or +CHA (charm/bluff/impress) — whichever fits the described approach. If it could go either way, let the player choose.

- **10+** — pulls it off as well as one could hope. If the danger or the action was especially dire, "as well as one could hope" might just mean avoiding the worst outcome.
- **7-9** — lesser success, cost, or consequence (see SKILL.md's tier guidance). Consider offering a choice between two options, or a chance to back down.
- **6-** — hard GM move. The danger arrives before/during the action, or the action succeeds with severe fallout.

**Adjudication notes:** State the danger and stakes out loud when calling for the roll ("that's Defy Danger — the danger is it notices before you land the stab"). If the stakes can't be articulated, it isn't Defy Danger — just narrate the outcome. Don't allow a nonsensical approach to substitute for the actual danger (charming an incoming arrow doesn't dodge it — describe the consequence and offer a real choice instead). A PC's action triggering both Defy Danger and another move always resolves as the other move.

### Aid

**Trigger:** Helping someone who has not yet rolled, or two+ characters making the same move at once.

**Roll:** None for the Aiding character. The GM picks one:

- The primary roller **can accomplish more than they could alone** (choose this when the action would be impossible, take much longer, or have much less effect, without the help), or
- The primary roller **gains advantage** (choose this when they could've done it alone but the help makes it easier).

Either way, the Aiding character is exposed to any risk/cost/consequence of the roll they supported.

**Adjudication notes:** "Can I help?" isn't enough — ask how, and if there's no plausible answer, Aid doesn't trigger. Involve the Aiding character in negative outcomes, even on a 10+; consider a soft GM move against them to show the help carried risk. A character offering unrelated information or expertise (rather than direct support of the roll) may be Knowing Things instead of Aiding.

### Clash

**Trigger:** Fighting in melee or close quarters against a foe that is (or is willing and able to become) an active threat back. If the foe can't or won't fight back, skip the roll and just deal damage.

**Roll:** +STR.

- **10+** — the maneuver works as expected (deal damage) and pick 1: avoid/prevent/counter the enemy's attack, **or** strike hard and fast for +1d6 damage but suffer the enemy's attack.
- **7-9** — the maneuver mostly works (deal damage), but suffer the enemy's attack regardless.
- **6-** — maneuver fails and the enemy's attack lands; or any other hard move that fits (a killing blow that still costs something, e.g. the fallen foe crushes a follower).

**Adjudication notes:** "Suffer the enemy's attack" isn't only damage — it's license for any aggressive hard move using the enemy (put them in a spot, burn a resource, invoke a monster move), and it should include damage whenever the fiction would actually hurt them. Roll the PC's damage before deciding what the enemy's attack looks like — let the numbers (armor, tags, who's left standing) inform the narration. One Clash roll covers a single attack that could hit multiple foes (via area tags or fictional framing), but roll damage separately per foe. When several PCs/followers gang up on one foe, one of them Clashes and the rest Aid; when multiple attackers land damage on one foe, roll one (usually the highest) damage die and add +1 per additional capable attacker, combining applicable tags.

### Defend

**Trigger:** Taking up a defensive stance, or jumping in to protect someone/something else. Triggers **proactively** (bracing before an attack lands) or **reactively** (stepping between an already-announced attack and its target — but only to protect *someone else*; reactively protecting yourself is Defy Danger or Clash instead).

**Roll:** +CON.

- **10+** — hold 3 Readiness (4 with a shield).
- **7-9** — hold 1 Readiness (2 with a shield).
- **6-** — proactive: escalate the situation aggressively. Reactive: the hard move is usually obvious from the attack already in play.

Spend Readiness 1-for-1, in response to a specific attack, to: suffer the attack's damage/effects instead of your ward; halve an attack's damage/effect; draw all attention from your ward to yourself; or strike back at the attacker (deal damage, with disadvantage). Each option can only be used once per attack, but multiple different options can stack against the same attack. Readiness is lost the moment the character goes on the offense, stops focusing on defense, or the threat passes.

**Adjudication notes:** Readiness can only be spent in ways the fiction supports — no interrupting an attack the character can't perceive or striking a foe out of reach. Halving reduces damage *before* armor. Striking back applies the character's damage tags but doesn't retroactively stop the attack being defended against. A character can trigger other moves while holding Readiness (that's the point) and keeps the Readiness as long as they stay defensively postured — fighting defensively (e.g., holding ground while Clashing) doesn't burn it, but charging in does.

### Interfere

**Trigger:** One PC acts, another PC tries to foil it, and neither backs down. (Does not apply against monsters/most NPCs; may apply against followers acting on a PC's orders, case-by-case.)

**Roll:** +STR/DEX/CON/INT/WIS/CHA — matching the interfering approach (same list as Defy Danger).

- **10+** — the acting PC picks 1: act anyway with disadvantage on their next roll (even an unrelated one), or relent/change course.
- **7-9** — same choice, but the Interfering character ends up off-balance, exposed, or vulnerable (open them up to retaliation, or put them in a spot).
- **6-** — Interference fails to stop the original action, which proceeds (triggering its own move if applicable) — the acting PC doesn't get to back down now, they already committed. Still counts as a hard GM move, so it's fair for the interference to "succeed" fictionally but at a much steeper price.

**Adjudication notes:** Pause and let both players consider backing off before rolling — either can concede without dice. This move never targets monsters, and rarely targets NPCs.

### Know Things

**Trigger:** Consulting accumulated knowledge about a topic — not for recalling common knowledge or details the table already established, which the GM should just supply directly.

**Roll:** +INT.

- **10+** — something interesting *and* useful (and true).
- **7-9** — something interesting; making it useful is on the player. If stuck, try: a useful hint that doesn't spell it out; two contradictory useful facts left undecided until tested; or a true-but-hard-to-act-on fact.
- **6-** — put them in a spot, introduce a new danger, reveal an unwelcome truth, offer a costly opportunity, or say they simply don't know. Optionally (with full table buy-in) tell them something they believe is true but that will burn them if acted on.

**Adjudication notes:** Players don't need to justify their knowledge before rolling — ask "how do you know this?" after the roll and let them invent the backstory. Say "no" to a topic sparingly — only when it's genuinely beyond the character's experience. Route simultaneous same-topic attempts through one roller with others Aiding.

### Let Fly

**Trigger:** Taking a ranged shot. If the shot is easy (safe, unpressured, target unaware/helpless — contextual to the character), skip the roll and deal damage directly. If it's tricky or the shooter is under pressure, roll first.

**Roll:** +DEX.

- **10+** — clear shot, deal damage.
- **7-9** — pick 1: deal damage but deplete ammo (mark the weapon's next status; skip this option if it has none); hold steady and wait for an opening, dealing damage once it arrives; move to a clear shot at the cost of exposure or lost ground, then deal damage; or rush the shot and deal damage, accepting a GM-chosen cost/complication.
- **6-** — GM decides, and it's rarely a clean miss: the shot could be interrupted before release, or land while triggering something bad, possibly hitting an unintended target nearby instead.

**Adjudication notes:** As with Clash, the roll determines whether/how the shot connects; the damage roll (with armor/tags) determines effect — a negligible roll is a graze, a negated one bounces off or is dodged. Always narrate the target's reaction regardless of numeric effect. One roll covers a shot that could hit multiple targets; roll damage per target. Warn a player before a 7-9 choice would itself require Defying Danger or lead to an unavoidable hard consequence, and let them reconsider.

### Persuade (vs. NPCs)

**Trigger:** Pressing or enticing an NPC to do (or not do) something specific, when the NPC has a reason — even an irrational one — to resist. No reason to resist means no roll; they just do it.

**Roll:** +CHA.

- **10+** — the NPC does as asked, or (if their approach wouldn't realistically be enough) reveals the easiest way to actually convince them.
- **7-9** — not convinced yet, but reveals a way they could be — ideally costly, tricky, or distasteful.
- **6-** — the NPC won't be swayed (possibly ending the scene), complies but with complications, or the whole exchange generates chaos/offense elsewhere (draw on Threat and Homefront moves for consequences).

**Adjudication notes:** Clarify the specific ask before calling for the roll. Convincing angles to draw on: a promise/oath, a safe/discreet opportunity, appeals to ego/honor/conscience/fear, a convincing lie, a strong offer, joint effort, threatened or actual violence, something wanted/needed, proof/corroboration, third-party pressure or permission — or anything else that fits. A revealed condition must be true, even if not easy; offering two alternative conditions is fine.

### Persuade (vs. PCs)

**Trigger:** Pressing or enticing a PC to do something, when the PC's player resists.

**Procedure:** The pressing player first asks the target's player, out of character: "Could I possibly get you to do this, yes or no?"

- **"No"** ends it — the pressing character must let it drop. (Ask the target's player how the refusal reads in the fiction.)
- **"Yes"** — the pressing player may then roll +CHA:
  - **10+** — target marks XP if they comply without further convincing; if they refuse anyway, they must reveal how they *could* be convinced.
  - **7-9** — target marks XP if they comply without further convincing; no XP if they counter-offer instead (even if they later accept their own counter-offer).
  - **6-** — GM's call: optionally let the target turn it around and ask how they could get the presser to do something, or interrupt the scene with an unrelated move. Never presume either character's feelings or reactions.

**Adjudication notes:** The "yes/no" gate exists specifically so the mechanics never override what a player has decided their character would or wouldn't do — a "no" is final, not an opening bid. **Persuade vs. Interfere:** use Persuade when a PC wants another PC to agree to something; use Interfere when a PC is already acting and another tries to stop them. A PC can Interfere against an attempt to Persuade them.

### Seek Insight

**Trigger:** Studying a situation or person and looking to the GM to reveal something beyond the obvious — the character must actually do something (subtle or overt), not just ask "what do I see?"

**Roll:** +WIS.

- **10+** — ask 3 questions from the list below; take advantage on the *first* roll made to act on any answer (not per question).
- **7-9** — ask 1 question from the list; same single-use advantage.
- **6-** — in tense/dangerous scenes, they're likely interrupted or surprised while studying; in safe scenes, announce new trouble, reveal an unwelcome truth, advance a looming threat, or burn a resource. Never remove agency outright, and never contradict how the player said they were studying the scene.

Question list: *What happened here recently? What is about to happen? What should I be on the lookout for? What here is useful or valuable to me? Who or what is really in control here? What here is not what it appears to be?*

**Adjudication notes:** Answer honestly, generously, and specifically — "nothing" or "no one's in control, it's chaos" are legitimate, useful answers. Scale specificity to how thoroughly the character actually investigated. A good off-list question can be answered directly, or redirected to the closest on-list question.

## Special moves

### Advantage/Disadvantage

Not a triggered move — a modifier that applies to another roll.

- **Advantage:** roll an extra die, discard the lowest (typically 3d6, keep highest two).
- **Disadvantage:** roll an extra die, discard the highest (typically 3d6, keep lowest two).
- **Both at once:** they cancel; roll normally.
- **On damage rolls:** advantage/disadvantage apply only to the main damage die (roll it twice, keep the higher/lower), then add any bonus dice afterward.
- **They don't stack.** Multiple sources of advantage are still just advantage; the same goes for disadvantage. A tie for highest/lowest when discarding can be broken arbitrarily.

Sources of advantage: a move that grants it (Aid, Seek Insight's follow-up), equipment/arcana, or a GM-offered opportunity. Sources of disadvantage: a move that imposes it (Interfere), a marked debility affecting the rolled stat, equipment/arcana, or a GM-revealed downside/consequence. Use on-the-fly advantage/disadvantage for difficulty sparingly — first consider whether the action even triggers a move, whether the stakes/risk should simply be adjusted, or whether the scope of a single roll should expand or shrink instead.

### Burn Brightly

**Trigger:** The character has enough XP to Level Up (6 + twice current level) but can't yet spend it (e.g., mid-expedition).

**Effect:** Spend 2 XP after any roll of their own (a move roll, a damage roll, even the Die of Fate) to add +1 to that roll — max +1 per roll, and only on the character's own rolls. This slows over-leveling and subtly favors players who've weathered a long stretch of setbacks. Watch for a rolled 6 or 9 and prompt the player to check whether they have XP to spend.

### End of Session

A structured end-of-session ritual, not a triggered move:

1. Each player names a moment their instinct came through (for good or ill); if uncontested, mark XP (max 1 XP/session for this).
2. Each player states how their relationship with or opinion of a PC, NPC, or group shifted; if uncontested, mark XP (max 1 XP/session for this).
3. As a group, answer four yes/no questions, needing full agreement (including the GM) for each "yes" — every "yes" earns everyone XP: *Did we learn more about the world or its history? Did we defeat a threat to Stonetop or the region? Did we improve our standing with our neighbors? Did we make a lasting improvement to Stonetop, or tangible progress toward one?*
4. Everyone (GM included) praises something they enjoyed from the session, in fiction or at the table.
5. Everyone (GM included) offers a wish for future sessions — about the fiction or about play at the table. The GM notes these down and works toward them.

### Death's Door

**Trigger:** The character is dying. (Full harm/healing context lives on page 245 of the book, outside this skill's scope.)

The player describes glimpsing the Last Door and the Lady of Crows, then rolls +nothing:

- **10+** — wrests back to life; return to 1 HP, and describe how the brush with death left a mark.
- **7-9** — the Lady waves them off: no longer dying, but out of the action.
- **6-** — choose 1: make one last move as if a 12+ result, then step through the Last Door (die); refuse to go and gain the Revenant or Ghost insert; or call on one of the Things Below by name and beseech it, gaining the Thrall insert.

## Out of scope

Follower moves (Order Followers, Strengthen Your Bond), Expedition moves (Chart a Course, Outfit, Requisition, Have What You Need, Recover, Forage, Keep Company, Make Camp, Struggle as One, Return Triumphant), and Homefront moves (Bolster, Convalesce, Deploy, Level Up, Make a Plan, Meet With Disaster, Muster, Pull Together, Seasons Change, Trade & Barter) are named in the source chapter but detailed elsewhere in the book — they're outside this skill. The general precedence rule still applies: a more-specific move beats a more-general one, and everything beats Defy Danger.
