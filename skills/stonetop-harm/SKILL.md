# Stonetop: Harm & Healing

Adjudicate the four types of harm a Stonetop character can suffer, and the moves that heal them. Use this skill any time a character gets hurt, marks a debility, picks up a lasting wound, hits 0 HP, or tries to recover between fights.

## The four types of harm

Any hurt in the fiction can produce one or more of these, layered together:

1. **Damage to HP** — loses vitality/plot armor, not "meat."
2. **Debilities** — Weakened, Dazed, or Miserable; mechanical disadvantage plus fictional color.
3. **Problematic wounds** — lasting, fictionally consequential injuries (a shattered knee, a severed hand, an infected cut).
4. **Death or a lethal wound** — 0 HP from a source that could kill triggers Death's Door.

Pick whichever combination the fiction calls for. A single hit can deal damage, force a debility, *and* leave a problematic wound.

## Mechanics layer

### HP and damage

HP are an abstraction — fighting spirit, luck, skill — not a wound tracker. 0 HP means out of the action, not necessarily "hurt." PCs generally have more HP than NPCs/monsters because they're protagonists.

Deal damage when: a player-facing move says so, you *hurt them*, you *use up their resources* (and HP is the resource), or any GM move involves them getting hit/roughed up/pained. If a prepared danger deals the damage, use its stated dice/tags. Otherwise, judge severity against what it'd do to a normal person:

| Likely effect | Damage die |
|---|---|
| Bruises, scrapes, pain, light burns | d4 |
| Nasty flesh wounds, bruises, or burns | d6 |
| Broken bones, deep/wide burns, debilitating pain | d8 |
| Death or dismemberment | d10 |

**Multiple attackers at once**: roll the single highest die among them, +1 per additional attacker; combine all their tags. (Six d6 attackers plus one d8+3 attacker = d8+9, with all their tags.)

**Armor**: subtract the target's armor rating from damage (can reduce to 0). `[n] piercing` ignores n points of armor for that hit only. `ignores armor` means armor doesn't apply at all (fire through chainmail, psychic attacks). Moves that say "lose n HP" (vs. "take n damage") always ignore armor.

**At 0 HP**:
- PC, potentially lethal source → Death's Door (below).
- PC, non-lethal source → out of the action (unconscious, panicking, trapped); can't self-rescue until someone helps or the GM says otherwise. Ask the player how/why they went down.
- NPC/monster → GM's call; potentially lethal source usually means dead soon, otherwise just out of the fight. Don't feel obligated to protect them.
- Followers are NPCs (GM decides fate) but the GM may let the player roll Death's Door for them instead.

**Regaining HP**: via Recover, Make Camp, a 10+ on Death's Door, Convalesce, another move, or a GM-offered opportunity. Non-follower NPCs/monsters regain HP at the GM's discretion — guideline: minutes of rest + supplies = 3-5 HP; a night's rest = half max; days of rest = full. Regaining HP ≠ wounds closing; it means they've caught their breath and have more fight left before the next lethal blow.

### Debilities

Three possible marks, each imposing disadvantage on two stats:

| Debility | Fictional state | Disadvantage on |
|---|---|---|
| Weakened | Fatigued, tired, shaky | +STR, +DEX |
| Dazed | Befuddled, can't think clearly | +INT, +WIS |
| Miserable | Distressed, unwell, in pain | +CON, +CHA |

Mark a debility when a move says to (prescriptive), or when the fiction would obviously leave someone weakened/dazed/miserable (descriptive) — e.g. from being *hurt*, a demonstrated downside, an opportunity-with-a-cost, or told consequences. When the player picks the debility, ask what it looks like in the fiction.

If a specific debility is already marked and something would mark it again, the character catches a break — no further mechanical penalty (fiction may still worsen). If a move *requires* marking a debility as its trigger and all relevant debilities are already marked, the move can't be triggered.

Only PCs (and, optionally, followers as a temporary tag) mark debilities — NPCs/monsters don't roll, so just narrate them as slower, shakier, etc.

Clear a debility via Recover, Make Camp, Convalesce, another move, or an offered opportunity — clearing means the mechanical penalty and active distress end, not that the underlying injury is healed (weakened-from-torn-bicep clears to "bandaged and in a sling," not "healed").

### Problematic wounds

A lasting, fictionally consequential injury — inflicted whenever the GM *hurts them* or makes a move where a character is hurt in a specific way. Just declare it true: a twisted ankle can't bear weight, a severed hand is severed and bleeding. These are especially common with **messy** attacks (destructive, often destroy gear too) and go hand-in-hand with **forceful** attacks (which shove/reposition even on a miss).

Problematic wounds almost always come bundled with HP loss, and often with a debility too. There's no lookup table for severity — follow GM principles and let the fiction (and, if undecided, the rolled damage) guide how bad it is. Good moves to hang a wound on: *use up their resources*, *announce future/offscreen trouble*, *reveal an unwelcome truth*, *put someone in a spot*, *demonstrate a downside*, *tell them the consequences*, *tell them the requirements*.

Problematic wounds are stabilized (not healed) via Recover with the right fictional treatment, and only fully close up (if they can) via Convalesce.

### Death and dying

A PC hits Death's Door when reduced to 0 HP by a potentially lethal source — including pure fiction with no damage roll (falling 100 feet onto rocks). Roll +nothing:

| Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 10+ | Back to 1 HP, no longer dying — but marked in mind/body/soul (player picks the mark; bring it back often, maybe as an affliction threat) |
| 7-9 | No longer dying, but unconscious/out of the action until the GM says otherwise |
| 6- | Choose 1: make one last move as if a 12+, then die; **refuse to go** (Ghost/Revenant insert, needs a Terrible Purpose); or **call on a Thing Below** by name (Thrall insert) |

Others can Aid the roll (tending wounds, etc.); the risks/costs depend on how they aid and the table's take on the Lady of Crows. The GM may hold off resolving the move until the scene wraps. On a 6-, there's no saving them with that last move, and only the rarest magic reverses it. A PC who "sticks around" as Ghost/Revenant/Thrall is on a horror-tinged, borrowed-time trajectory — their Terrible Purpose (or master, for a Thrall) becomes a threat the other PCs should be wary of.

### Healing moves — quick comparison

| Move | Timeframe | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Recover** | A short breather | 1 use of supplies | Regain 4+Prosperity HP (once per damage-taking); describe how you tend a debility/wound — GM says it's handled or names a requirement (Defy Danger, more supplies, find X, Make Camp, drastic measures) |
| **Make Camp** | Rest in an unsafe area | 1 use of supplies/provisions per person (mess kit feeds 4 on 1 use) | If fed/watered/slept: pick 1 — regain ½ max HP, or clear a debility. Bedroll adds +1d6 HP regardless of choice. Great rest also grants advantage on the next roll (rare) |
| **Convalesce** | Days-to-weeks in safety/care | Time, safety, a healer for wounds | Full HP, clear all debilities; weeks under a healer heals problematic wounds that *can* heal. Permanent injuries force retire-or-Make-a-Plan-to-adapt |

Recover never heals wounds or regrows anything — it stabilizes. Make Camp never resolves problematic wounds. Only Convalesce can close a wound, and even then not permanent ones (lost limbs, shattered joints) — those go to Make a Plan.

## In-play layer

**Narrate harm as consequence, not spreadsheet math.** Don't just say "take 6 damage" — say what happens: the club catches a rib, the blade opens a line across the forearm, the fall wrenches a knee. HP loss is wear-and-tear (bruises, ragged breath, rattled nerves) unless the move specifically *hurts them* — reserve visible, described wounds for those moments so they land.

**Let approaching 0 HP create tension, not certainty.** A PC at 5-6 HP should feel one bad roll from Death's Door — lean into that dread in your descriptions — but don't narrate every hit as near-fatal from full HP; that cheapens the moments that matter.

**Use problematic wounds to complicate, not just to punish.** They're a tool for GM moves — *announce future trouble*, *reveal an unwelcome truth*, *tell them the requirements* — that keep an injury alive in the fiction across scenes (an infected cut that worsens, a knee that gives out at the worst time). A wound that's inflicted once and never mentioned again wastes the tool.

**Reserve messy/forceful consequences for the attacks tagged that way.** Messy attacks are your cue to hurt-them-hard and threaten gear; forceful attacks should shift position or momentum even when they whiff on damage. Don't hand out problematic wounds indiscriminately — save the nastiest ones for messy hits or for when a 6- demands real fictional cost.

**Debilities are roleplay prompts as much as penalties.** When a player marks one, ask what it looks like — that answer is what you play back to them in scene (the miserable character grousing, the dazed character struggling to track the conversation). A debility can justify calling for Defy Danger where you otherwise wouldn't.

**Pace healing with the fiction, not the clock.** Recover fits mid-scene lulls and keeps a fight or expedition moving without erasing its cost (one use per HP-loss event, no farming). Make Camp is the between-encounters reset on the road — spend the GM's Make Camp questions to advance downtime fiction, not just to hand out HP. Convalesce is a season-turn: use it to skip ahead, ask homefront questions, and let permanent injuries become real character turning points (retire, or Make a Plan and adapt) rather than pure setbacks — you're a fan of these characters, not their obstacle.

**Death's Door is a spotlight moment, not paperwork.** Describe the Lady of Crows and the Last Door in a way that fits your table. It's fine to hold the roll until the scene (or another PC) gets its beat first. On a 6-, respect the finality — no using the last move to dodge death — and if a player wants to stick around as Ghost/Revenant/Thrall, make sure they can articulate the Terrible Purpose or name the Thing Below before you grant it.
